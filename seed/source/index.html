How we started

Meridian Coastal began in 2011 with one 40-litre blending drum and a standing order from the cafe next door. We still blend in the same building, though the drum has been joined by three others and the standing order has grown to fourteen wholesale accounts along the coast.

Resting is the part most producers skip. It lets the moisture in a fresh blend equalise across the leaf, so the third cup from a tin tastes like the first. Nothing leaves the shed without a batch card recording the garden, the blend date, and the name of whoever mixed it.

Sourcing

We buy from six partner gardens and visit each at least once every two years. Four are smallholder cooperatives; two are single estates. We pay above the regional auction average and publish what we paid, per lot, in the annual letter that goes to wholesale accounts each March.

Brewing notes

Water just off the boil for the black blends; let greens cool to around 80 °C — a minute off the kettle is close enough. Three grams of leaf to 200 millilitres, three minutes for the first infusion.

Most blends reward a second and third infusion, each about thirty seconds longer than the last; the oolong will take five. If a cup comes out thin, add leaf rather than time. If it comes out harsh, drop the water temperature before you shorten the steep.

Wholesale

We supply cafes, restaurants, and a handful of independent grocers along the northern coast. Minimum order is two kilograms across any blends, delivered fortnightly on our own van within forty kilometres and by courier beyond that. Batch cards travel with every delivery.
